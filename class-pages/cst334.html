CST 334: Operating Systems

Research Project | Barrelfish: A Multikernel Operating System

Collaborated with a team to research Barrelfish, an experimental operating system that explores a multikernel architecture for modern multicore computer systems.

We analyzed the research paper's motivation, architecture, scalability, and approach to communication and resource management across processor cores. The project connected Barrelfish's design to operating system concepts covered throughout CST 334.

Our team produced a research summary and video presentation explaining the system's design, experimental findings, and significance to modern operating systems.


Course Reflection | Applying Operating Systems to Full-Stack Engineering

CST 334 gave me a deeper understanding of what happens beneath the applications I build. Throughout the course, I learned about processes, threads, concurrency, synchronization, memory management, file systems, virtualization, and how operating systems manage hardware resources.

As a full-stack engineer, this knowledge helps me think beyond simply making an application work. Understanding processes and concurrency gives me a better foundation for building backend services that handle multiple requests efficiently, while concepts such as memory and resource management help me recognize potential performance and scalability problems.

The course also changed how I think about servers, containers, and cloud infrastructure. Technologies used in modern full-stack development rely heavily on operating system concepts such as process isolation, virtualization, networking, and resource allocation. Having this foundation will help me make better architectural decisions as I build and deploy larger applications.

Overall, CST 334 helped connect lower-level computer science concepts with the higher-level web development work I enjoy. I plan to apply these concepts by building full-stack applications that are not only functional, but also efficient, scalable, and reliable.
